## Overview

Nigeria's recorded diabetes prevalence is lower than Canada's, 3.0% versus 7.7%. Yet its age-standardised diabetes mortality rate is **3.2 times higher**, a gap that has widened over 23 years as Canada's rate declined while Nigeria's showed no sustained downward trend. This divergence points to health system performance gaps rather than disease burden alone. An estimated **78.7% of people with diabetes in Nigeria remain undiagnosed** and are therefore largely outside the reach of formal care. Nigeria has made substantial investments in primary health care infrastructure, including the expansion of the NPHCDA network and the deployment of approximately 43,000 Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs), providing a foundation on which targeted adaptations can be built.

## Central Finding

Nigeria's recorded diabetes prevalence is lower than Canada's - 3.0% versus 7.7%. Yet its age-standardised diabetes mortality rate is 3.2 times higher, a ratio that grew from 1.7× in 2000 to 3.2× in 2023 as Canada's rate declined and Nigeria's fluctuated without a sustained downward trend. This divergence points to structural gaps in detection, treatment access, and care continuity, not differences in clinical knowledge or therapeutic options.

## Key Findings

- Nigeria's age-standardised diabetes mortality rate: **28.5 per 100,000** (2023) vs Canada's **8.8** — a ratio of **3.2×**, up from 1.7× in 2000
- Approximately **78.7%** of people with diabetes in Nigeria are undiagnosed, compared to 28.9% in Canada
- Nigeria has **40,990 diabetes deaths annually** — against a backdrop of lower recorded prevalence
- Three system-level gaps drive the divergence: **diagnostic coverage, treatment access, and continuity of care**
- Only **25.1%** of Nigerian diabetics receive treatment vs **67.0%** in Canada — driven by financial inaccessibility (71.9% out-of-pocket expenditure) and structural workforce constraints (3.8 vs 28.19 physicians per 10,000)
- WHO evidence indicates that up to **~80%** of preventable medication errors in low-resource settings originate at the prescribing stage
- Canada's connected health information investment generates an estimated **$1 billion** in annual health system value

## Three Evidence-Based Recommendations

| Recommendation | Maps To | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **DETECT** | Low diagnostic coverage | National diabetes registry built on NPHCDA infrastructure · CHEWs as frontline screening agents |
| **REACH** | Limited treatment access | Community-based treatment delivery through CHEWs — reducing both geographic and financial barriers to sustained care |
| **CONNECT** | Continuity of care gap | Digital health records at every PHC facility enabling longitudinal patient tracking across providers |

## Analytical Framework

The analysis follows the diabetes care cascade across five stages: prevalence, diagnosis, treatment, sustained care, and mortality. Age-standardised rates are used for all bilateral mortality comparisons. Cascade indicators are drawn from multiple sources with different denominators and reference years — the cascade is treated as a directional framework showing where patients are lost, not as a mathematically continuous pathway. All findings reflect associations between health system indicators and mortality outcomes. No causal mechanisms are established. Limitations, denominator differences, and uncertainty intervals are documented in full on the Methods page.
